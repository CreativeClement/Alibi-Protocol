How it works

Use it like a dashcam. Open Shield only when you need it.

1

Open Drive and grant location

The map uses OpenStreetMap. Your position is shown on the road. Nothing records until you start dashcam.

2

Start dashcam for the trip

The rear-facing camera captures the road. Keep the phone mounted. Stop recording when the drive is over — clips go to Vault on this device.

3

If you are pulled over, tap SHIELD

Legal Shield keeps the road camera and adds microphone (and in-cab video if you allow it). End Shield when the encounter is over.

4

Review in Vault, then After

Export or delete clips locally. Use After for know-your-rights language and national legal-help directories.

Legal Shield

Built for the stop — not for a light show.

Police strobes and cinematic rain do not help anyone in a real encounter. Shield is a recording mode: road video, officer audio, optional cabin camera, and a file you control.

What Shield does

Starts or continues dashcam, requests microphone access, and can open the in-cab camera. Preview of the cabin feed only appears while that camera is live. There is no automatic start, no fake network feed, and no animation that distracts from the road.

Recording laws vary by state. Audio consent rules differ. This product does not give legal advice.

Why it exists

A record belongs to the person who lived it.

Alibi Protocol started as a response to how easily an encounter can be reduced to someone else’s narrative. The first version is deliberately local: your phone, your vault, your export.

“I built this so a driver has a record of what happened — on the road and in the cabin — without handing that record to a platform by default.”

Timothy Clement · Founder · Concord, New Hampshire
